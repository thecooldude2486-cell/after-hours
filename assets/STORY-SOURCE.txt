You play as Eli Ward, a 17-year-old who returns to an abandoned school at night after receiving a voicemail from his younger sister Mara.
The problem is that Mara died in that school eight years ago.
The voicemail is only seven seconds long:
“Eli… you said you’d come back.”

When they were children, Eli and Mara attended the same school. Eli was protective of her, but one afternoon they had an argument. Mara was being bullied and begged Eli to stay with her after school.
He was angry.
He told her:
“You’re not my responsibility.”

He left.
That night, a fire broke out in the old east wing.
Mara never came home.
Her body was never found.
Eli has spent eight years telling himself there was nothing he could have done.
Then the school calls him back.
The school should feel personal, not just haunted
At first, the school looks abandoned.
Peeling paint. Rain leaking through the roof. Old lockers. Water dripping into buckets left decades ago.
But the deeper Eli goes, the more the building begins reconstructing itself from his memories.
He walks into an abandoned classroom.
The lights flicker.
Suddenly it is daytime again.
Children are laughing.
His old teacher is writing on the board.
Mara is sitting two rows ahead.
She turns around.
Smiles at him.
Then everything goes dark again.
Her chair is empty.
That should be the rhythm of the horror: terror → quiet → memory → grief → terror.
The scary scenes matter because the player becomes desperate to reach the next peaceful memory of Mara.
The main relationship
Mara should remain present throughout almost the entire game.
Not physically at first.
She speaks through:
- classroom phones
- old cassette players
- PA speakers
- walkie-talkies
- messages written in notebooks
- childhood drawings
- eventually, directly to Eli
At first she sounds like the little girl Eli remembers.
“Do you remember where we used to hide?”

Later, she sounds older.
That is strange.
Because Mara died at nine.
Eventually Eli realizes something heartbreaking:
Mara has experienced the eight years too.
She has been trapped inside the school the entire time.
To Eli, she disappeared eight years ago.
To Mara, she has been alone for eight years.
The monster
I would remove most of the separate monsters.
Have one central creature.
The Teacher
Nobody remembers her real name.
She appears as an impossibly tall teacher wearing burnt, melted clothing.
She constantly searches the halls calling:
“Mara?”

At first, the player assumes she is hunting Mara.
Later you discover the truth.
She was Mrs. Vale, the teacher who went back into the burning east wing to find Mara.
She died trying to save her.
The monster isn't really evil.
It is Mrs. Vale's final instinct repeating forever:
find the child.
Sometimes she attacks Eli because she believes he abandoned Mara.
She whispers things like:
“You left her.”

“She waited.”

“You went home.”

That becomes much more disturbing than a monster simply roaring at you.
Chapter 1 — The Voicemail
Eli enters the school during a storm.
He tells himself he is only there to prove the voicemail is some cruel prank.
He finds Mara's old locker.
Inside is a bracelet he made for her as a child.
Impossible.
He buried that bracelet with his mother after she died.
Then the school intercom activates.
Mara's voice:
“You took a long time.”

The front doors will no longer open.
Chapter 2 — Don't Leave Me
Eli follows Mara's voice into parts of the school from their childhood.
The player learns about their relationship through small things instead of exposition.
A drawing:
ME + ELI VS THE WORLD
A teacher's note:
“Mara seems highly dependent on her older brother.”

An old photograph of Eli holding Mara's hand on her first day.
Then another memory plays.
Young Mara:
“Can you wait after school today?”

Young Eli:
“I've got things to do.”

“Please?”

“Mara, stop.”

Silence.
The memory ends.
The first time The Teacher appears is immediately afterward.
Chapter 3 — Eight Years
Eli finally communicates properly with Mara through an old classroom telephone.
This should be one of the game's first truly emotional scenes.
He asks:
“Where are you?”

Mara says:
“Still here.”

Eli asks:
“Are you hurt?”

A long silence.
Then:
“Not anymore.”

Eli starts crying.
“Mara… I'm sorry.”

And Mara responds:
“For what?”

That destroys him.
Because she doesn't even know that he has spent his whole adult life blaming himself.
Later in the conversation, something feels wrong.
Mara references things that happened after her disappearance.
She knows their father moved away.
She knows their mother died.
She knows Eli never finished university.
Eli asks how.
Mara says:
“I hear things when people come here.”

And then:
“I heard Mum looking for me.”

You later discover their mother had repeatedly broken into the abandoned school after Mara disappeared.
Mara heard her calling.
But could never answer.
Chapter 4 — The Fire
Eventually the school forces Eli to relive the night Mara disappeared.
Not through a cutscene.
The player plays it.
You become 17-year-old Eli again.
No monsters.
No supernatural effects.
Just an ordinary school afternoon.
Mara has been bullied.
She asks Eli to walk home with her.
But Eli has plans with friends.
The player is forced to walk away.
You can turn around.
You can try to return.
But an invisible boundary stops you.
Mara stands at the end of the corridor.
She says:
“Eli?”

You keep walking.
“Eli?”

The hallway stretches longer.
“Please don't leave me.”

Then the fire alarm begins.
Now the player understands why Eli came back.
He isn't really trying to save Mara.
He is trying to change the last thing he ever did to her.
Chapter 5 — What Actually Happened
The big reveal should be quieter than the original version.
There was no government experiment.
No giant conspiracy.
The school burned because of an electrical fault.
But something beneath the building — perhaps an ancient geological anomaly, something supernatural that is never fully explained — reacted to the fear and grief of the people trapped inside.
Their final moments became embedded in the building.
Most disappeared over time.
Mara didn't.
Because she kept waiting for Eli.
Her final thought wasn't fear.
It was:
He'll come back.
That belief became so strong that the school could never let her go.
And therefore it could never let the fire end.
The entire haunting exists because a little girl kept believing her brother would return.
And eventually…
he did.
The most painful revelation
Eli eventually finds Mara.
Not nine-year-old Mara.
She appears around seventeen.
The age she would have been if she had lived.
Eli just stares at her.
She smiles nervously.
“Do I look weird?”

Eli can't answer.
She asks:
“What?”

And Eli says:
“You grew up.”

Mara:
“I think I did.”

That could be the emotional centre of the entire game.
They finally hug.
No monster.
No loud music.
Just Eli holding the sister he hasn't touched in eight years.
Then Mara says:
“I knew you'd come back.”

And Eli breaks down.
The final tragedy
Eli believes he can take Mara out.
For the final section, they travel through the collapsing school together.
Mara becomes an actual companion.
She squeezes through gaps.
Helps unlock doors.
Makes little jokes.
Talks about ordinary things she never got to experience.
She asks:
“What's driving like?”

“Did Dad get grey hair?”

“Do people still use Facebook?”

At one point she asks Eli:
“Do you think I'd have been pretty?”

Eli responds:
“You would've been annoying.”

Mara laughs.
“I hate you.”

Eli:
“Yeah.”

Pause.
“I missed you too.”

You let the player love having her around.
Which is exactly why the ending hurts.
The ending
They reach the front entrance.
Sunrise is coming.
For the first time, the doors open.
Eli walks outside.
Mara stops.
Eli:
“Come on.”

She doesn't move.
“Mara?”

She already knows.
She cannot leave.
She is not alive.
Everything the player has done wasn't about rescuing her.
It was about allowing her to stop waiting.
Mara begins crying.
Not because she is afraid of dying.
Because she realizes what Eli is going to do.
Eli walks back inside.
Mara:
“No.”

He hugs her.
She keeps saying:
“No, no, no. Go.”

Eli says:
“I already left you once.”

Mara:
“You were seventeen.”

“You were a kid.”

Eli:
“So were you.”

And finally Mara says the thing Eli has needed to hear for eight years:
“I never blamed you.”

The school starts collapsing.
Eli takes her hand.
Mara asks:
“Are you staying?”

Eli shakes his head.
“No.”

Then:
“We're leaving together.”

He walks with her deeper into the school instead of toward the exit.
They enter the classroom from their childhood.
The room is no longer burned.
Morning sunlight comes through the windows.
Mara is nine again.
Eli is seventeen again.
They sit next to each other.
Outside, children are going home.
Mara:
“Can we go now?”

Eli smiles.
“Yeah.”

The school bell rings.
For the first time in the entire game, it sounds normal.
They walk out of the classroom together.
Cut to the real world.
The abandoned school stands silent.
The front doors slowly open.
But nobody comes out.
On the floor is Eli's phone.
Its screen lights up.
One new voicemail.
The player hears Mara laughing.
Then Eli:
“Come on, slowpoke.”

Mara:
“Wait for me!”

Eli:
“I am.”

Black screen.
AFTER HOURS
Some people don't haunt places because they died there.
They haunt them because they were waiting for someone to come back.
